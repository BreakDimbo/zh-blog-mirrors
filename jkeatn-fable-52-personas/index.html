这几个星期，我一直在用 Fable 5.2 在代码里画画。关于 Opus 5.5 的画，我听到最多的批评之一，是它不过是在模仿已有的艺术。我觉得这说得对，于是就请 Fable 5.2 去长出一些「人设」——带着某种来历、风格也大致能落在某个时代里的画家。

我并不知道会走到哪里，却看着这个项目朝我从未预料的方向长开。我给一点评语或转向，Claude 就会更新或改写驱动笔刷和颜料的引擎。遇到我喜欢或觉得有意思的画，我就让它再往深处走。下面是一些我比较喜欢的。

每一张都是一个 Python 程序，有的长达一两千行，每一笔都要经过一个模拟的绘画引擎，笔毛也不尽相同。多数只要几千笔，我也试过推到高得多的笔数，结果有好有坏。Claude 没有使用图像模型，也没有事先渲染或描摹。

英文原文

for the past few weeks, I’ve been exploring painting with Fable 5.2 in code. one of the most common critiques I heard about Opus 5.5’s paintings was that it was only emulating real art. I thought that was fair, so i’ve been asking Fable 5.2 to develop personas—artists with some sort of a back story and a consistent style roughly identifiable in time

not knowing what to expect, i’ve watched this project evolve in directions i never predicted. I give comments or steers, and Claude updates or revises the engines that power the brushes and paint. where paintings come up i like or find intriguing, i ask it to go deeper. these are some favorites.

each of these is a python program, some one to two thousand lines of code, that places every stroke through a simulated paint engine with different bristles. most take a few thousand strokes, but i’ve pushed much higher with varying results. Claude does not use an image model, nor render or trace in advance

I’ve been impressed with its consistency. these are the paintings of a persona Fable 5.2 developed, “Dermot Hale,” who “paints weather in thick oil with a knife and big brushes, usually in unscenic places like a retail park or a drive-in, at the moment just before it changes. His stated reason: ‘it is large, it is on time, and nobody is in charge of it.’”

it likes aerial views. I was particularly taken with a persona claude called “The Tidewright”

I asked Fable 5.2 to create a painting as indistinguishable as possible from a photograph. this is roughly 700,000 simulated brush strokes

when i left Fable 5.2 to choose its subjects, some topics recurred. it likes light, hands, and people’s work or people working. it gave me quite a few airplanes, wind turbines, and data centers, and appears to find traffic quite interesting

i’m far from having a scientific opinion on this work. LLM text has been studied enough that we understand some basins, but it’s a harder task to map what happens when a model has to make hundreds of thousands of choices like these. there’s a lot to explore with respect to model character and personality, with code and image understanding, but i have been extremely impressed by how models can render, in code and without other tools, imagined worlds (if still with occasionally awkward anatomy and topsy-turvy physics). mostly though, my main takeaway is that far more than is common with claude’s writing, i find some of these quite beautiful
